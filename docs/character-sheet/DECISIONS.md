## 2026-11-16 — Casters get a second right-rail column

**Ruled.** The owner, 2026-11-16: "Add a 2nd column to the right rail for casters with the top being the entry and the 5 below being dynamic trackers of active spells, I will reconsider later after more user testing."

**Why.** The party cell's menu held all summon management — per-spell *Cast* and *Dismiss*, a loose cast's *Dismiss*, *Dismiss all*, *Summon…*, *Bind* and *Release*. A character marching in a formation whose party token is on the scene has that cell routed to the formation sheet, so none of it was reachable.

**Done.** How the column and the menus work is the model's (MODEL, "The cast column"). Release and Summon… moved from the party menu to the cast menu; the party cell and its formation routing are unchanged.

**Bind retired.** Asked whether a creature bound by hand joins its binder's party, the owner, 2026-11-16: "What is bind? Sounds like a legacy shortcut ready to retire." *Bind* (2026-09-03) marked the controlled tokens as a character's summons; it predates summoning through spells (2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11), and is removed. *Release* stays, for a creature a sheet already shows as bound. This supersedes in part the 2026-09-03 right-rail ruling's "The party menu is where a Judge binds one", on the owner's word above.

**Choices.**
- A tracker's time left is `effectClock`, the Effects tab's own reading, so the cell agrees with the row its press opens; a record the module has announced as ended takes no cell, since that cast is over and its card has been posted.
- More than five casts: the fifth cell stands for the rest as `+n` (the count it hides) and opens the same tab. Rejected: a sixth overflow row, because the column is the portrait's height.
- A seat that does not own the character sees the column and its trackers, with the entry as a pad that opens nothing.
- The column's trigger is a spell item, a caster-side record or a summon standing on the scene, not the Magic tab's test (`spells.enabled` or a casting pool), so a character holding a spell on a sheet with spellcasting switched off still reaches its casts.

**Supersedes in part**, within seven days of the ruling it changes ("The party cell's summoning rows are one per spell", 2026-11-11), on this evidence: a character marching in a formation whose party token is on the scene has its party cell routed to the formation sheet, so the rows that ruling put in the party menu cannot be reached. That was not known then.

**Cost.** A summon standing on the scene gives its summoner the column too, so *Dismiss* and *Release* stay reachable from a sheet with no spell. A character with no spell, no cast record and no summon standing cannot be given a bare *Summon…* from its own sheet; a Judge summons for it from a caster's, or gives it a spell.

## 2026-11-16 — A summon marches in its summoner's party

**Asked.** The owner, 2026-11-16: "Minor UI bug, summons and party formation conflict on the sheet. I propose a new button for casting that includes summons management. Then you can see them in you henchmen management when not in a party as currently, but when in a party they get added to the full formation." Asked whether summons become real formation members (stashed into the party token, marching in the order, deploying with it) or are shown and counted while staying on the map: "Real members". Asked whether a summon at 0 hp inside the party is a casualty or not a person: "Not a person, removed/destroyed."

**Ruled.** A creature a summoning cast made joins its summoner's formation while the summoner rides inside a party token on the creature's scene: at the cast, at the summoner's joining and at a deployed summoner's recall. It leaves with the summoner. It ends when its token or its member record goes, and at 0 hit points, stashed or deployed, as a dismissal ends it; it is never a person, a casualty or a load. Dismissal and a cast's end reach it in either state, and the party cell, the cast menu and the Magic tab count it as standing on its party's scene. A creature bound by hand has no cast record and is never gathered as a summon. How it is built is formation MODEL, "A summon is a member under an id of its own", and its identity ruling is formation DECISIONS of the same date.

**Supersedes in part**, within seven days of two of the rulings it changes ("A summon is an unlinked token of a GM-made copy", 2026-11-10: "goes when its token goes"; "The party cell's summoning rows are one per spell", 2026-11-11: what standing on the scene means), on this evidence: the owner's ruling above, which neither had. It also qualifies the 2026-09-03 right-rail ruling's premise that "a formation's members are people in a marching order, not a summoner's creatures"; that ruling's rejection of the asterisk count stands.

**Cost.** A Player acts on a summon held in a party only through *Dismiss* and its summoner's membership: its row on the party sheet takes no action. A cast's `tokenUuids` names tokens deleted once the creatures join, so a reader of that list finds them gone.

## 2026-11-18 — Gold and pale mark the granted throws on the Rolls tab

**Ruled.** The owner, 2026-11-18, of the rows the Rolls tab draws for a proficiency a spell grants: "pale when not cast and can cast on self. Gold when cast on self even if by another." Of a spell the character cannot cast: "can't cast does not show as can but uncast". Of the chat card a roll posts: "Rolls in chat should match the group roll format, just maybe with 1 line if that's applicable."

**Qualifies** "Six tones, one meaning each" (2026-09-03, "The design canvas is the specification"): gold stays specialization and achievement, and also marks a granted throw a record holds on the character, whoever cast it. The tone means the same elsewhere; this adds one row state to one tab. It also qualifies, for a granted throw alone, the abilities feature's "A proficiency throw wears the system's own chat card" (archived in the abilities DECISIONS-through-v11.14.5): that throw posts a one-line party card instead.

**Done.** The behavior is the model's (MODEL, "Rolls", the paragraph on granted throws). Proceeded on as defaults, not the owner's words (spell-effects plan): a known spell with no slot left still shows pale (1c.25), a pale row is display only (1c.26), two spells giving one throw make one pale row naming both (1c.28), and a granted throw posts the one-line card (1c.31).

**Rejected.** `.is-disabled` or `.is-off` for the pale state: both already mean a suspended effect on the Effects tab.

**Cost.** A pale figure is what the register's reach builds at the caster level its source resolves; a cast may differ once the caster picks an option or types another level. The row id no longer carries the record's id, so two records granting one throw are one row, as the stacking already made them one figure.

## 2026-11-19 — A pile's row unfolds to Take, and Open is the Judge's

The owner, on the third board of the set-down design: "Approve, build it". The
board put a list under each pile row on the Equipment tab's Kept elsewhere, one
line per row the pile holds with a Take button (a number box on a stack), on the
owner's row and on a bystander's.

- **Closed by default**, a real button with `aria-expanded`, its state kept in the
  sheet and not in the document: a long pile would otherwise push every other
  place off the tab, and a redraw must not shut what a player opened.
- **Every row the pile stores is a line**, the container and what is in it,
  flat, as the board draws them: a bystander may take one torch out of a pack
  without taking the pack.
- **Open is the Judge's.** It opens the pile's own sheet, whose Storage tab a
  Player cannot see; offered to a Player it opens a window with nothing in it.
- **A bystander's row is listed from reach alone**, read at each render; a token
  move re-renders nothing (2026-09-22, in force), so the row follows the next
  render.

**Not offered:** a Take button on a line the viewer neither owns nor reaches. An
always-open list is the board's own alternative to the fold, should the fold test
badly live.

*Cost:* a bystander who walks up to a pile does not see its row until the sheet
next renders.
