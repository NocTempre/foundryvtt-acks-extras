## 2026-11-13 — The column is spaced as the book spaces it

**New evidence.** The owner's rulings: 2026-11-05 "follow RAW not the grid";
2026-11-12, asked how a deploy lays a spaced column, "Book spacing where it
fits". And the importer now reads the three printed figures (RR ch. 6,
"Combatant Frontage and Interval"): `marchFrontageFeet`, `wideFrontageFeet` and
`rankIntervalFeet` in the `formation` document. This supersedes IN PART "The
party token is the head of its column" (2026-11-04), whose column stood one
body per cell and whose deploy took the cells, and "The column stands inside
its walls" (2026-11-04), whose wall test and deploy rule were per cell. Both
stand unchanged for a world with none of the three imported.

**Ruled.**
- Spaced only when all three figures are above nothing and the scene says what a
  cell is worth; otherwise one body per cell, byte for byte as before, with no
  notice, no setting and no fallback figure.
- Rank r stands r × interval along the line through the spine cells' centres;
  files spread right then left at the sum of their half-frontages; a file is
  open when its far edge stays on the scene and no movement wall lies between
  it and the body before it, and a refused body goes to the next rank.
- A body is wide when it is an individual with an equipped melee weapon that is
  large and deals bludgeoning or slashing damage and none that pierces, read
  across the weapon's base and alternate damage. Everyone else, stacks and
  blanks, marches. The piercing exclusion is an inferred reading: core carries
  a size and damage types on a weapon, and the rule's own wording is read
  against those; a Judge whose reading differs gives the weapon another size.
- Deploy is the owner's "Book spacing where it fits": a token's footprint, its
  own width and height centred on its point and shrunk a pixel, stands there
  when no wall that stops movement touches it (an open door is not one, a
  one-way wall counts by its line) and it lies on the scene; else on the body's
  one-per-cell cell. Overlap with another body is not a misfit. A stack's bodies
  are one cell each. A spaced deploy and restore always lay the column, since
  the points have no straight block to fall back to.
- `trailBound` grows past one cell per body only when the rank interval is wider
  than a cell. The drawn square is the body's frontage wide, and the column's
  lights, the mapper's place and the carried light points read the points.
- A recall of a spaced deploy re-forms the party token on the cell corner
  nearest the first recalled token, since a token at a point is off the grid.

**Rejected.** Importing corridor widths: the walls the scene draws are the
corridor. A notice or a setting for the not-imported case: the surface is the
cell column the module has always drawn, and nothing in it claims to be the
book's. Deploying to the point regardless of a wall: a token inside rock.
Deploying only on the cell, as the book's points are not cells: that
discards the owner's ruling for the sake of the grid.

**Cost.** A world imported before this release holds none of the three until
Rules tables is run again in Reimport One Shelf with the books open; until
then nothing changes. Spaced tokens stand between cells until a user moves one,
and a drag snaps it to the grid. The `columnLights` and `columnPlacement`
shapes change (points and `{point, cell}`), both internal. `effectiveFrontage`
and the trap readers of rank depth are left to the trap-ranks slice.

## 2026-11-13 — Trap ranks read the laid column — SUPERSEDED IN PART 2026-11-13 (a rank's depth no longer falls back to a constant)

**New evidence.** The column is laid by the book's frontage and interval
(the entry above), so the rank a body stands in is not its member's index
over the frontage: a blank square and every body of a stack take ground, a file
a wall squeezes out stands a rank back, and a rank is the imported interval
deep. The owner's rulings: 2026-11-05 "follow RAW not the grid"; for this phase,
"Fixes in, new features out."

**Ruled.**
- `runTrapCheck` and `sweepForTraps` read `laidOrder` (`formation-model.mjs`):
  the marching order's rows with rank and file taken from the seat of each
  member's first body in `columnFor`. It answers `marchingOrder` where no column
  is laid (no party token on the map, a hex, a gridless or mile-scale scene).
  `marchingOrder` stays the published contract and no trap reads it.
- A rank's depth is `rankFeet`: the imported `rankIntervalFeet` where the scene
  is spaced, else `FEET_PER_RANK`, so a world with nothing imported reads the
  depth it always did. `victimsOf` takes it as an option and `searcherReachFeet`
  as an argument; a figure that is no length reads as the default. The probe
  sequence is unchanged.
- `effectiveFrontage`, the seam the squeeze was to arrive through, is deleted. It
  had no caller; the squeeze is the column's own wall test, which reaches the
  trap through `laidOrder`.

**Rejected.** A second walk of the column inside the trap code: `columnFor` is
the one place that knows which file a wall refused. Measuring a victim by the
distance between laid points: an area's radius is read in ranks as it always
was, and only the depth of a rank changes.

**Cost.** A column with blanks or stacks now meets a trap in a different rank
order than the per-member count gave, which is the fix. The search, pole and
trap-breaking reaches and the other readers of `FEET_PER_RANK` stay constants
for the trap-ranks slice (T-0419). A trap check reads the party token's stored
position and trail.

## 2026-11-13 — The trap reaches, the trigger die and the pit's dice are imported

## 2026-11-13 — Probes in one square are shuffled, and a body precedes a pole

**Ruled.** Characters marching abreast step onto the same ground at the same moment and each throws separately (RR p. 267). The order among probes that reach the same square is shuffled, because the sequence ends at the throw that springs the trap and a fixed file order would make whoever stands in file 0 spring every trap. The shuffle is per square and per kind, so ranks still meet the trap front to back. Where a pole and a body reach the same square the body goes first: someone already walking that ground got there before a pole was waved over it. The pole still throws separately, but what it buys is untrodden ground ahead of the column, not precedence over a body already in the square.
**Rejected.** A fixed file order (leftmost first): it is not the book's and it biases every trap onto one file.

## 2026-11-13 — A pit's spikes are a nested dice count, not a product

**Ruled.** A spiked pit adds `(count)dX`, where the count is itself a dice formula: the victim lands on a rolled number of spikes that each deal their own damage, which is that many separate dice. Foundry's parser resolves the nested count first, so the formula is the rule and not an approximation of it.
**Rejected.** Multiplying one die by the count: it has a different spread, flatter and far swingier at the top.

## 2026-11-13 — A conveyance in a city carries no rate

**Ruled.** A litter or a wagon affords privacy and is not any faster, so a conveyance on the settlement board is a flag the Judge can see (`private`) and carries no block rate. Being unseen is the Judge's call.
**Rejected.** A conveyance that changes the block rate: it would invent a rule the book does not state.

## 2026-11-13 — The city's travel tables register as `cityTravel`, not `settlement`

**Ruled.** The settlement derivations read the registered document `cityTravel`. The henchmen feature already registers a `settlement` document for market class by families, and a settlement in this codebase means a place where people live.
**Rejected.** Sharing the `settlement` id: it would put two unrelated features' tables in one document and make "is the settlement data imported?" an ambiguous question.

## 2026-11-13 — Who goes short when the packs cannot feed everyone is a setting, and even is the default

**Ruled.** The book does not state who goes short when the packs cannot feed everyone: it prices what hunger does, never whom a captain feeds. The choice is therefore a world setting (`provisionSharing`), not a rule, and the kinder reading, `even`, is the default.
