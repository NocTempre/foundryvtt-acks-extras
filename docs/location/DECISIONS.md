## 2026-11-13 — A Judge named as an owner is not one

**Asked** (owner, 2026-11-13): "should a GM listed as an owner stop counting
toward deposit/coin reach in this release?" **Answer, verbatim:** "Fix now
(Recommended)". This reverses 2026-10-10, which ruled that it stays and that
leaving Judges out of `ownersShare` is roadmap work. For example: a Judge
creates a character and a warehouse; both carry that Judge at OWNER by
Foundry's own stamp; the character, standing nowhere near it, no longer reaches
the warehouse until it is pinned, stood at, or a player is given OWNER on it.

**Ruled.** `ownersShare` skips an owner whose user is a Judge (core's `isGM`,
which includes the assistant role), read from the user the ownership map names
and never from the seat. An id that names no user counts for nobody. A Judge
stands for no character, so sharing one says nothing about whose a place is.

**Evidence (what 2026-10-10 did not weigh).** The answer depended on who pressed
Create: the same character and place were in reach or not by which seat made
them, with no pin, token, link or vault in play. The stamp also widened through
`companionOwns`: a player's character marching beside a Judge-made NPC reached
every place that Judge made.

**Rejected:** a `game.user.isGM` bypass (the seat then decides, 2026-09-15's
defect again). **Rejected:** counting `GAMEMASTER` only, which keeps the stamp
alive for an assistant who creates. **Rejected:** stripping the stamp at create,
which reaches no document made before the release and fights core's own rule.
**Rejected:** a setting (a toggle on a gate whose right answer is not in
dispute). **Rejected:** auto-pinning what a Judge makes (writes player-facing
state nobody asked for).

*Cost:* a Judge-made character stops reaching an unlinked, non-pinned place a
Judge also made, and a Judge-only NPC stops reaching any unlinked place on
ownership alone, so deposits and coin acts that passed on the stamp now answer
`notYours` or `outOfReach` in a running campaign. The way back is a Judge's act:
pin the place to the character, stand at it, or give the player OWNER on it.
Retrieval stays ungated, and a Judge's own coin acts still pass.

## 2026-11-13 — A dropped actor means what the tab it lands on says

**Ruled.** On the recruitment tab a dropped NPC is somebody the party can hire; anywhere else it is somebody who is simply here (an occupant: the innkeeper, the garrison, the stabled mule). Both readings are right in their own tab and wrong in the other, so the drop target arbitrates. A location dropped on a location is nesting whichever tab it lands on, through the cycle guard.
**Rejected.** A modifier key to choose the reading: nobody would find it.

## 2026-11-13 — The stash picker moves everything in one transfer call

**Ruled.** Everything picked in the stash dialog moves in one transfer call, because a single move that fails halfway is far easier to reason about than twenty that each might. A container is flagged in the picker so nobody is surprised that its contents travel with it.

## 2026-11-13 — The storage manager writes direct and is last-write-wins

**Ruled.** The manager is a GM tool, so its writes go direct with no sockets. Two GMs driving it at once is last-write-wins on distinct items, which is documented rather than defended against.
**Rejected.** The socket-and-claim-token machinery: it exists for player-triggered flows, not for two people sharing one admin screen.

## 2026-11-13 — A market's till float is stored, and a null one derives once

**Ruled.** `system.market.tillTargetGp`, the till float a market keeps in coin, is stored and never a buried derivation, because it is a knob other systems turn. Null means derive once and write it back: families × income from the imported tables, a placeholder setting otherwise. The month sweep tops the house coin up to it, and `tillRefreshTime` is that sweep's watermark.

## 2026-11-13 — The Judge's record of a place is a second field, not a gate on the shared notes

**Ruled.** `system.gmNotes` is a second field beside `system.notes`, so the two are visibly different records instead of one field whose audience depends on a setting; the notes are shared. It renders only inside the sheet's GM region, which is the whole of its protection: the same UI convention the roster's `hidden` follows, not a security boundary.
**Rejected.** A gate on `notes` that varies its audience by a setting.

## 2026-11-14 — A city gate is the point of entry to the market it stands inside

Asked "Every place offers a Role of "City gate" on its Contents tab, and
settlement imports set it, but nothing in the module reads it. Keep the
control or drop it?", the owner answered: "Fix the gates to follow RAW rules
for entry to a town/city."

**Ruled.** The rules print one entry procedure, the market's (RR ch. 8,
Entering the Market, RR 370–372), and its step 3 is charged at the point of
entry, which the page says a city gate is. The markets feature already
charges that step, so the role is made to lead to it rather than to charge
anything of its own. A place with the `gate` role is the point of entry to its
nearest ancestor with a market. Its sheet links that market and opens the
market's Ventures tab. Standing at it counts as standing at the market, inside
the one definition of reach, so the entry the gate offers is never refused at
the gate, and the next day's trading from the same spot is not either. This
supersedes in part "A place has a role" (2026-09-30): its "settlement board's
gate action" is not built and is not the role's reader.

**Rejected:**
- Gate hours, shutting at night, the watch's word, a refusal at the gate: no
  page prints them, and no house rules ship.
- Entry allowed only through a gate. The page names gates among other points
  of entry, and a market nobody drew a gate for must stay enterable.
- A reach clause for market entry alone. That would be a second definition of
  reach, refusing the next day's trade at the spot where entry was allowed.
- A port or caravanserai role beside the gate. The mechanic is the same, and a
  Judge marks one as a gate.
- Recording which gate a party used. Nothing printed varies by gate.

*Cost:* every gated market has a second body for deposits and coin as well as
entry, since reach is one rule. A world's gates are walked once per reach
sweep, or once per call where a caller holds no scan.

## 2026-11-14 — A market's class is the Judge's or the table's, and otherwise unknown

A place with a market and no `marketClassOverride` and no urban families read as
a class the module chose, and so did a family count below the settlement table's
smallest bracket. Both defaults are gone:
`system.marketClass` is the override, else the imported table's class for the
families, else `null` (the owner's "Missing imports stay blank with page
reference as a subtle clue", and "a figure a page prints is imported"). The
class badge on the sheet is then blank with the page reference (RR 352) as its
tooltip, and no search fee shows.

- **The session's call, owner may overrule: the 1 to 6 range stays.** The
  clamp and the override field's bounds are the count of the grid's class
  columns, structure and not a printed rung.
- **The session's call, owner may overrule: a count below the smallest bracket
  is unknown.** The page prints one more settlement row below the recipe's
  first, a class with a footnote that confines it to a domain's stronghold, so
  its answer is conditional and no single class names it. `table-recipes.mjs`
  does not read that row; reading it would be a recipe change, and a row the
  getter could then return only where the place is a stronghold. Until then
  that count answers null.
- **The session's call, owner may overrule: `hasMarket`, not the class, says
  whether a place is a market for coin.** `exchangeTermsAt` and the till read
  `marketClass != null` as "has a market" only because the class had never
  been null for one; neither uses the class, so both read the subtree's
  presence.

Rejected: a blank class option on the override select that stands for a
default; a "class not set" notice (the owner's ruling is a blank field and a
page reference, no text saying it is missing). *Cost:* a market a Judge adds
and does not describe does nothing in henchmen and trade until a class is set;
a world that relied on the fourth class sets it once.

## 2026-11-16 — A place's parent is its filing, not where its token stands

The native-regions audit inferred that a place's quarter, copied into
`system.parentUuid` when the place is made, goes stale once its token moves.
Checked against the code, the copy is not a position: every reader of
`parentUuid` walks the hierarchy (the sheet's breadcrumb and contents,
`gateMarketOf`, `domainOf`, `factionsAt`, `cityStampOf`, the prune's
`emptyOfPlaces`), and every reader that needs position already asks geometry
(`travelToPlace`, `districtPlace`, `districtUnder`). It stands on "Places
nest" (2026-08-02) and on the stored record winning over a live observation
(`docs/lib/PLACES.md`). The owner's rulings the audit served: "The new actor
types like districts are a UI for the more complex needs, not a replacement
for a fundamental object type", and "Design principles, composition (extend)
better than bespoke": the Region answers where a token stands, and the
place's parent answers where it is filed.

Rejected: repointing the readers at the token's Region, since none of them
means the token's ground; and re-filing a place when its token moves, which
overwrites the Judge's filing and has no single answer for a place with
tokens on several maps. *Cost:* a Judge who drags a promoted place into
another quarter sees the breadcrumb keep the old one until they re-file it by
a drop on the sheet.

## 2026-11-18 — A pack set down is a pile on the ground, and at risk — SUPERSEDED IN PART 2026-11-19

**Ruled** (owner, 2026-11-18, intake row T-0653), on load, verbatim: "no, but it
is on the ground and at risk". On the approved design: "Take recommendations",
then "Build it now". For example: a character
wearing a Backpack with torches, 40 gp and a dagger inside sets it down. The
pack and everything in it leave them for a pile at their token and their Load
drops; the pile is listed with their goods kept elsewhere as at risk; picking it
up puts the pack back on their Back with whatever is still in the pile.

**Superseded in part 2026-11-19:** who may take from the pile. The design left
it open and its recommendations assumed only the character's own rows; see
"Anyone standing at a pile may take from it" below.

**Settled by "Take recommendations"** (the design's recommended option on each
point):

- The container and everything inside it move whole to a ground pile at the
  character's spot, through lib storage's `stash` (1B).
- At risk means only that the pile is reachable and stays where it is. The module
  runs no loss or theft check (2A).
- Any carried container may be set down, a sack included (3A; RR ch. 4,
  Sack, Large).
- Per character only (4A).
- Pick up restores the worn slot, with whatever is still in the pile (5A).
- Pick up is not reach-gated, as every retrieval (6A; 2026-08-06).
- The pile is a location carrying `flags["acks-extras"].ground`, owned by this
  feature, which is the sub-type's one owner (2026-07-19). It is made on the
  Judge's seat and authorized on the user who asked. See MODEL, "On the ground".

**Qualifies 2026-09-16, "A point of interest is a place's token, not a note".**
That ruling makes every location's token a point of interest. A pile is a
location with a token, and filing it as one would put a deployed wagon under it
and offer it as somewhere to walk to. `standsAsPlace` leaves a pile out and
nothing else about the 2026-09-16 ruling changes.

**Rejected:** a pile owned by the player, or left at the location default of
OWNER (it would take deposits from anywhere and from companions, since
`ownersShare` counts a place's default level). **Rejected:** creating the pile
on a Player's seat (a Player may not create an actor or a token, or delete an
actor). **Rejected:** removing an empty pile by prune (prune keeps a provider on
purpose, so removal must be a hook). **Rejected:** relying on `keepId` to keep
the container's id (both legs of a transfer mint new ids), so the record carries
the old one. The unchosen options of the six questions were not kept with the
design, so none are listed here.

*Cost:* the pile's contents are kept at a place, so a payment under the
`all` coin scope can draw on coin set down in a pack, and a Judge who deletes a
pile still holding goods gets them back inside a wrapper container with no slot
to return to. Landing on another character's sheet never restores a slot.

## 2026-11-19 — Anyone standing at a pile may take from it

**Ruled** (owner, 2026-11-19, intake row T-0653), asked "can another player's
character take straight from someone else's pile?", verbatim: "Yes, anyone
there". Earlier rulings stand: "no, but it is on the ground and at risk";
"Take recommendations"; "Build it now". The approved design said anyone at the
pile can take from it. For example: Bran sets down his Backpack. Alys's player,
whose character stands at the pile, takes Bran's rope from it with no Judge
involved.

**Supersedes in part 2026-11-18**, on who may take. *New evidence:* the owner's
answer to the question that entry's design left open; before it the only rule
written was lib storage's, which lets a seat take rows stamped to its own
character.

- A character that reaches a pile (`depositReach`, its own token) takes any
  row on it, through lib storage's relayed retrieve; the Judge is not asked.
- A character away from it takes only rows stamped to them, so Pick up stays
  reachable from anywhere (2026-11-18, 6A) and a stranger cannot take at a
  distance.
- Every other provider keeps the rule it had.
- A pack taken by a bystander lands on them loose: its record is cleared and
  the worn mark that rode the transfer is taken off. The pile goes once it
  holds nothing.

**Rejected:** a pile owned by its owner or by the players, so that ownership
carries the right to take (it would also take deposits from anywhere, as
2026-11-18 rejected); a Judge's approval per take.

*Cost:* a character can lose a pack's contents to anyone who walks up, which
is the at-risk the owner ruled for; the module runs no theft check. The pile's
own sheet does not yet show a bystander take controls; that surface waits on
the owner's design approval, and until then the take is the relayed API call.

## 2026-11-25 — The table default for a new place stops at the importer's stamp (T-0813)

**Ruled.** A new location whose creation data names no ownership default is opened to the
table at Owner (`preCreateActor`). One route brought an imported place to that default with
its imported text on it: the importer's write to the Actors tab where a line's compendium
cannot open (`createDoc`, which announces it), since the importer's data names no ownership.
`placeOpensToTable` (lib `place.mjs`) now answers the hook: creation data that names a
default, or that carries the importer's stamp (`cookbookId`), is left at core's own default,
None, which is the level every world copy the importer makes itself already carries. A place
made by hand carries no stamp and is opened to the table as before. A place dragged out of a
compendium was never on this route: core 14's import names the default None itself
(`WorldCollection#fromCompendium`), and the hook yields to a named default.
[A place's notes answer to ownership](DECISIONS-through-v11.14.5.md#a-places-notes-answer-to-ownership-not-to-the-judges-seat-2026-08-05)
stands; this decides only which places start owned. The shape was settled between the
sessions working the 2026-11-25 batch of reports and has not been put to the owner.

**Rejected.** Dropping the table default for every place: a place a Judge makes at the table
is the party's shared record, and the default is what makes an inn or a vault's hall usable
without an ownership dialog each. Reading the compendium source (`_stats.compendiumSource`)
in place of the stamp: the importer's write to the Actors tab carries no source. Naming the
ownership in each of the importer's place builders: the next builder written would take
the table default again.

**Cost.** An imported place the importer wrote to the Actors tab is closed to players until
the Judge opens it (Actors tab, right-click the place, Configure Ownership), and one written
there before this change is still the table's. An imported place is now stored in its
compendium with a default of None where it was stored with Owner; core reads a compendium
document's level from the compendium, so nothing reads the difference.

beside this one, so a stamped place the entry above leaves at None was opened at Owner all
the same.

**New evidence.** The walk `formation-hidden-place`, run against the entry above as
landed, read an ownership default of Owner on a place made with the importer's stamp and no
named ownership. The copy dragged out of a compendium read None in the same run, since
core's import names that default and both hooks yield to a named one.

**Qualifies** henchmen's
[Recruiting needs no GM client online](../henchmen/DECISIONS-through-v11.14.5.md#recruiting-needs-no-gm-client-online-2026-07-22):
a place made by hand is still opened to the table, by this feature's hook, and the routing
that ruling describes is unchanged. Which feature writes the default follows henchmen's
[The location sub-type belongs to the location feature](../henchmen/DECISIONS-through-v11.14.5.md#the-location-sub-type-belongs-to-the-location-feature-2026-07-19).

**Cost.** None beyond the entry above: the place its Cost line describes is now the place
the module makes.

## 2026-11-25 — A link needs every end it writes

**Authority** (owner, 2026-11-25, verbatim): "Tool permissions are hardcoded (e.g. summons)
by server role instead of object ownership, only judge secrets are supposed to be an
exception to this rule". With it, on who owns what (T-0793): "Spell owner can setup the spell
as needed. Trusted players own their sheet and instances of items within. I guess regular
Player only gets ownership of the sheet itself and not subitems they don't own? In either
case the approach is follow foundry permissions, don't invent new ones." The owner's standing
line on automation stays: "players get blocks, judges get warnings".

**Ruled.** Linking or unlinking a scene or a Region to a place (`linkScene`, `unlinkScene`,
`linkRegion`, `unlinkRegion`, `scene-link.mjs`) answers to ownership of every document the
call writes, and not to the seat's role. A link writes the scene's (or Region's) flag, the
place's mirror, the mirror of the place the scene named before, and the scene the place held
before; the call refuses unless the seat owns each of those that it would write. A scene
that is a part of its place writes its flag alone and needs the scene alone. A refusal is a
warning (`ACKS-LOCATION.place.linkNotOwner`), changes nothing, and the function answers
`false`; a caller that must know whether a link landed reads that answer. Foundry answers
OWNER on every document for a Judge, so a Judge passes with no role read. The same test
draws the sheet's Unlink control (`canUnlinkScene`: the place is editable and the scene it

**Cost.** A scene linked to a place the Player does not own cannot be moved by that Player
